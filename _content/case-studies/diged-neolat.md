# Lucina Digital Edition

## Context and research question

A Neo-Latin poetry collection of 1474, held as a manuscript in Madrid, is to be published as a scholarly digital edition. The edition has to do three things at once: present a text whose Latin most readers cannot check against the facsimile unaided, identify the historical persons and places the poems name, and make the metre visible, since metre is evidence in a Neo-Latin corpus. The case asks how far an agentic workflow carries an edition of that kind from a word-processor file.

## Data

The point of origin is a set of word-processor and PDF files carrying transcription and apparatus, with further texts alongside them. Nothing in that origin declares editorial structure; the division into poem, verse, apparatus and named entity had to be established before any encoding could follow.

## Approach

The work runs in successive published stages, and each stage is a separate deployed edition. The first converts the source files to TEI and renders static HTML. The second analyses the result and repairs it against TEI validation. The third builds the interface. The fourth integrates the footnotes and the critical apparatus. The fifth adds the prosopography, the metrical analysis and an editing mode. Prosopographical extraction and metrical analysis were run across more than one model, since neither task has a single reliable answer.

## Methodological contribution

The published stages are the contribution. Each is a working edition, and the sequence is inspectable, so a reader can see what the edition could do at each point and what the next iteration added. That is unusual: an edition project normally publishes one state and keeps its intermediate stages in a repository, where the progression stays invisible to anyone outside.

## Limits

The paper does not carry this case, and the site holds it because the edition stages document the method's progression. Any claim drawn from it stands on the repository alone.

Prosopographical identification and metrical analysis are the interpretive core of a Neo-Latin edition, and both were produced with model assistance. What the edition publishes at those two points therefore needs the same scrutiny a first-time editorial hypothesis needs, and the stages do not record which identifications a human confirmed.
