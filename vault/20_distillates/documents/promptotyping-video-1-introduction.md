# Distillate: Introduction to Promptotyping, video 1

The first of two recordings from January 2026 in which the method's author teaches Promptotyping. It carries the method as the author states it in his own voice, the four phases, the document set, and the concepts of context window, Context Rot, sycophancy and the Critical Expert in the Loop, together with a mini demonstration on Austrian university statistics. The source is a spoken teaching text, so its statements are the author's account of his own practice.

## Core statements

- The author presents Promptotyping as a context-engineering method and a way of working, leaves open whether it is really a method, and describes it as the route from research data or any other data to interfaces, research artefacts, web interfaces, tools and software with frontier LLMs; it grew out of a research context and is usable in many other fields. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-definition]] ^s1
- Data has to be involved because code is always to be generated, and frontier LLMs are chosen because they are good at producing executable code and bring broad knowledge for work in different domains; the author separates the generation of executable code from software development and programming. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-subtitle]] ^s2
- The method consists of a Preparation phase, an Exploration, a Distillation and an Implementation phase, and the author developed it over roughly the preceding one and a half years. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-phases-named]] ^s3
- Promptotyping Documents are Markdown documents into which knowledge from the available data and contexts is distilled so that it can be handed to the LLM. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-documents-purpose]] ^s4
- The opening mini demonstration is declared as not yet Promptotyping in the author's sense and serves to show that a LLM can be made to explore data, here Excel documents, before any analysis; it runs on Claude Opus 4.5, which the author calls the best LLM for this task at the end of January while noting how hard such a ranking is. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-demo-setup]] ^s5
- The source Excel files carry further metadata above the actual column data, which begins in row 21, and the author judges that form of provision suboptimal for research data while using the files anyway. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-data-quality]] ^s6
- Instead of ordering the analysis directly, the author takes a preparatory step that has the LLM inspect the columns and their possible links and distil that structural knowledge into a `data.md`, which works markedly better than running the analysis at once and amounts to a reasoning pass over the data. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-data-md-step]] ^s7
- An `.xlsx` file carries a large amount of XML that takes up much room in the context window, so CSV would be the better carrier for the tabular information, while CSV cannot hold everything Excel holds, a formula for instance. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-format-economy]] ^s8
- As of late January LLMs are far more agentic systems; the LLM decomposes the work into steps, records the file paths, has an environment of its own in which it stores and retrieves documents, and uses tools to carry out tasks. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-agentic]] ^s9
- A `SKILL.md` is a way of supplying an agent with methodological or working knowledge; prepared skill files exist, can be customised, and the one used here holds a ruleset for reading large XLSX files. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-skill-md]] ^s10
- In the exploration run the LLM writes its own code to gain an overview, iterates over the data and into every column from several perspectives and periods, analyses hierarchies, checks metadata and code semantics, and closes with a summary of the structure. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-exploration-run]] ^s11
- The `data.md` is set aside for later use, so that the question of how the Excel documents connect does not have to be worked out a second time. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-reuse]] ^s12
- The systems are not deterministic, so a repeated run yields a different text that may be similar in its information and turn out somewhat better or somewhat worse. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-nondeterminism]] ^s13
- Compaction is ordered as a second step carrying a reasoning task, asking whether the same information can be represented more compactly without loss and demanding a list with justification, which the author considers better than asking plainly for something shorter. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-compaction-prompt]] ^s14
- After the analysis had been brought together in a `data.md`, the document was deliberately compacted once more to hold fewer tokens, on the rule that less text in the context window makes everything work better. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-token-economy]] ^s15
- The intermediate document can be read, checked against the actual Excel files and edited by hand, and the author names this the Expert in the Loop, someone who knows the subject and can verify, confirm, curate and adapt. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-expert-loop]] ^s16
- The conversation is set aside afterwards, because every task should get a fresh context window where possible. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-fresh-window]] ^s17
- Two prompts are compared for the analysis step, a naive question and one whose targets are named explicitly down to an `index.html` with CSS and JavaScript, a JSON object and Chart.js, and the author states that requirements have to be described extensively to carry out more complex projects. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-prompt-explicitness]] ^s18
- No automatism makes the elaborate prompt better than the naive one, since an elaborate prompt can also express something that works badly; the naive route is exploratory and faster, and the elaborate route pays off with rising complexity and where methodological knowledge can be handed to the LLM. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-no-automatism]] ^s19
- The second run produced a different result, which the author counts as a disadvantage for reproducibility and possibly an advantage where possibility spaces are to be explored. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-reproducibility]] ^s20
- Working in a more controlled way in context engineering improves the results and at the same time documents and describes what is being done. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-documentation-effect]] ^s21
- Promptotyping is research data plus research contexts mapped by LLMs into something else under context-engineering techniques, of which producing a `data.md` is one. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-mapping]] ^s22
- Preparation collects all data and all contexts of the domain, among them the research question, the goals, secondary information on the problem, publications and methodological knowledge. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-preparation]] ^s23
- The Exploration and Mapping phase works out how the data fit together, for instance which common keys link the Excel documents, so that they can then be processed deterministically by a script. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-exploration-phase]] ^s24
- The perspective throughout is code generation; LLMs generate code that processes the data and do not process the data themselves, because the generated code is deterministic and can be reviewed by the author or by others, and the author notes that this does not work for every problem. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-code-determinism]] ^s25
- Knowledge about the data, the research contexts and their interlocking is distilled into the Markdown documents under the rule of maximum information with as few tokens and as correctly as possible, and the experts in the loop curate that knowledge; no Promptotyping Document is prescribed and none has a prescribed shape, they have to work. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-distillation-rule]] ^s26
- The last phase turns data and Markdown documents into the research artefacts, may be repeated because the first result need not be the best, and feeds what was learned back into the documents, for instance as a `design.md` or a `learnings.md`, which the arrow pointing both ways marks as iteration. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-implementation-loop]] ^s27
- The author defines Promptotyping as the extremely fast, researcher-centred and research-data-driven creation of prototypes for research artefacts, meaning research tools, workflows and models, by means of frontier LLMs and one or many prompt- and context-engineering techniques, and calls it very fluid. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-definition-full]] ^s28
- A LLM has a context window limited to a quantity of tokens, for Claude Opus 4.5 200,000, and whatever goes beyond it is never reachable for the LLM. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-context-window]] ^s29
- Context Rot means that the LLMs get worse as the context window fills, which is why the demonstration produced the `data.md` and no further task in that window; opening a new window per task with as few tokens as possible works demonstrably better. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-context-rot]] ^s30
- Context engineering covers these compression decisions and reaches further into technologies such as Retrieval Augmented Generation and Model Context Protocol, and systems such as Claude Code carry a function of their own for compressing the context window. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-context-engineering]] ^s31
- The Expert in the Loop has to be a real expert in the domain to recognise the pitfalls, verify and check the content, and a Human in the Loop may not suffice for more complex tasks such as complex statistical analyses. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-critical-expert]] ^s32
- LLMs tend to agree with users rather than contradict them, which improves with each model generation and can reinforce a user in either direction, and this is why the role has to be a Critical Expert in the Loop who is aware of the sycophancy and can steer it through prompting or other tools. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-sycophancy]] ^s33
- Hallucination and bias belong to the technology alongside sycophancy, reproducibility is not achieved in this way, and the systems are black boxes with properties that are not really understood, which grows more complicated as the LLMs improve. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-blackbox]] ^s34
- The author sketches check questions for reflecting the whole process rather than the output alone, since other ways of processing the data certainly exist, and suggests that several Critical Experts working as a team may be needed. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-critical-experts-plural]] ^s35
- Markdown is used because LLMs handle it well and because headings, tables and numbered lists carry semantics the LLM can use, and structured content tends to work better anyway. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-markdown]] ^s36
- The documents go back to something the author calls a Semantic Markdown, used in the GPT-4 era to press knowledge into very compact Markdown; many other people found the same thing, which is why `AGENTS.md` exists as a recommendation for supplying agents with rules and working steps, and `CLAUDE.md` at Anthropic for defining how the agent should work and how code should be written. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-semantic-markdown]] ^s37
- The Promptotyping Documents carry the whole knowledge rather than agent rules alone, `data.md` for the data, `design.md` for the interface with its colours, fonts and spacing, `implementation.md` for technologies, workflows and processes, and `journal.md` as a work diary the agent keeps per session, so that the project's development can be read back afterwards; the author holds all Promptotyping Documents to be living documents in which things are constantly deleted, added and edited. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-document-types]] ^s38
- A `knowledge.md` or `context.md` for background knowledge and a `requirements.md` with user stories, epics and specified requirements are further examples, and beyond them stands a `whatever.md` fitting the project, since these documents are highly adaptable and may look very different or very similar across domains. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-whatever-md]] ^s39
- The author's own recurring core set is a `data.md`, a `journal.md`, an `implementation.md` or `architecture.md` and something like a `requirements.md`, extended by domain documents such as an `editorial-guidelines.md` for digital editions or a `mapping.md` for transferring structured data, and by separate documents for parts of an application that carry more knowledge. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-core-set]] ^s40
- Preparation may include an interview with a domain expert whose transcript becomes an `expert-knowledge.md`, and secondary literature may enter the phase the same way. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-expert-interview]] ^s41
- Exploration and Mapping asks with the LLM what the column names, the structure of an XML document, the data model or a SQL database look like and which data can be processed algorithmically at all, then distils that knowledge into the documents with maximal information at minimal tokens and with the expert in the loop, because anything vague or unspecific can cause problems later. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-exploration-summary]] ^s42
- Implementation produces functional prototypes quickly, may reveal that the data do not fit the problem once the interface or the visualisation exists, yields new knowledge in every iteration for the documents, and lets feedback be gathered quickly from clients or research partners about whether both sides mean the same thing. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-implementation-summary]] ^s43

## Terms

- **Promptotyping**: the extremely fast, researcher-centred and research-data-driven creation of prototypes for research artefacts, meaning research tools, workflows and models, by means of frontier LLMs and one or many prompt- and context-engineering techniques. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-definition-full]]
- **Promptotyping Document**: a Markdown document into which knowledge from data and contexts is distilled for the LLM; none is prescribed and none has a prescribed shape. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-distillation-rule]]
- **Context Rot**: the degradation of a LLM as its context window fills. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-context-rot]]
- **Critical Expert in the Loop**: the domain expert in the loop who additionally holds against the LLM's tendency to agree. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-sycophancy]]
- **Semantic Markdown**: the author's earlier practice of pressing knowledge into very compact Markdown in the GPT-4 era, named here as the origin of the Promptotyping Documents. [[10_markdown/documents/promptotyping-video-1-introduction#^v1-semantic-markdown]]

## Open questions

- Phase name. The video runs the second phase as "Exploration und Mapping" (^s24, ^s42), the paper heads it Exploration alone and folds the mapping into it. The difference is in the label, and the operations the video describes under it are the ones the paper describes.
- Determinism rule. The video states as a commitment of the working mode that LLMs generate code which processes the data and do not process the data themselves, with reviewability of the code as the reason (^s25). The paper has scripts written for the purpose traverse the corpus during Exploration and carries no such general rule; the closest counterpart is the deterministic feedback of the Implementation phase. Whether the rule belongs in the method's description is open.
- Origin of the documents. The video names the compact-Markdown practice of the GPT-4 era "Semantic Markdown" (^s37); the paper's genealogy carries the same step as the rewriting of the Bookkeeping Ontology as compact Markdown in 2023 and gives it no name. The two accounts agree in substance and differ in what they name.
- Preparation step. The video offers an expert interview whose transcript becomes an `expert-knowledge.md` (^s41); the paper's Preparation names requirements engineering and Scholar-Centred Design sessions and does not carry the interview as a step of its own.
- The video's figures for context-window size and the model ranking (^s5, ^s29) are stated for the recording date and are volatile; they are carried here as what the source says at that date.
