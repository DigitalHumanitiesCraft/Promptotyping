# Distillate: Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier (Dell'Acqua et al. 2023)

A pre-registered field experiment with consultants at Boston Consulting Group, measuring the performance effect of GPT-4 access on realistic consulting tasks and naming the uneven boundary of AI capability a jagged technological frontier; the paper cites it as the property of the capability that stands beside the two failure modes. Quotes were checked against the openly hosted working-paper PDF (MIT Sloan copy of the SSRN version, abstract id 4573321), which carries the Harvard Business School Working Paper 24-013 cover.

## Core statements

- The study proposes that AI capability forms a jagged technological frontier, on which some tasks are easily done by AI while others of seemingly similar difficulty lie outside its current capability. ^s1
  > "We suggest that the capabilities of AI create a “jagged technological frontier” where some tasks are easily done by AI, while others, though seemingly similar in difficulty level, are outside the current capability of AI." (dellacqua-2023, §Abstract)
- The pre-registered experiment involved 758 consultants, about 7% of the individual-contributor consultants of the company. ^s2
  > "The pre-registered experiment involved 758 consultants comprising about 7% of the individual contributor-level consultants at the company." (dellacqua-2023, §Abstract)
- On 18 consulting tasks inside the frontier, consultants using AI completed 12.2% more tasks, finished them 25.1% more quickly, and produced results of more than 40% higher quality than the control group. ^s3
  > "For each one of a set of 18 realistic consulting tasks within the frontier of AI capabilities, consultants using AI were significantly more productive (they completed 12.2% more tasks on average, and completed tasks 25.1% more quickly), and produced significantly higher quality results (more than 40% higher quality compared to a control group)." (dellacqua-2023, §Abstract)
- On a task selected to lie outside the frontier, consultants using AI were 19 percentage points less likely to produce correct solutions than those working without AI. ^s4
  > "For a task selected to be outside the frontier, however, consultants using AI were 19 percentage points less likely to produce correct solutions compared to those without AI." (dellacqua-2023, §Abstract)
- Inside the frontier AI complements or displaces human work, and outside it AI output is inaccurate, less useful, and degrades human performance. ^s5
  > "Within this growing frontier, AI can complement or even displace human work; outside of the frontier, AI output is inaccurate, less useful, and degrades human performance." (dellacqua-2023, §1 Introduction)
- Knowledge work commonly runs as a flow of interdependent tasks of which some fit current AI and some do not. ^s6
  > "Most knowledge work includes this sort of flow, a set of interdependent tasks, some of which may be good fit for current AI, while some are not." (dellacqua-2023, §1 Introduction)
- Professionals find it hard to grasp where the boundary of the frontier runs, because AI capability evolves rapidly and is poorly understood. ^s7
  > "However, because the capabilities of AI are rapidly evolving and poorly understood, it can be hard for professionals to grasp exactly what the boundary of this frontier might be at a given." (dellacqua-2023, §1 Introduction; the sentence breaks off in the working paper as printed)

## Terms

- **Jagged technological frontier**: the uneven boundary of AI capability, along which task difficulty as a human judges it fails to predict whether AI handles the task. [[20_distillates/publications/dellacqua-2023-jagged-frontier#^s1]]
- **Centaurs and Cyborgs**: the two patterns of successful human-AI integration the study observed, division of labour between human and AI against continuous interleaving of the task flow with the AI. [[20_distillates/publications/dellacqua-2023-jagged-frontier#^s1]]

## Open questions

- The experiment measures consulting tasks under a firm's own task design. Whether the same jaggedness appears in research work with sources and data is not tested by this source, and the paper's transfer to that setting goes beyond it.
- The study reports one task selected to lie outside the frontier, so the size of the negative effect rests on a single task and the quoted 19 percentage points carry no distribution across outside-frontier tasks.
- The frontier is dated to GPT-4 as of 2023. Where the boundary runs for later models is outside the source, and the paper uses the shape of the finding rather than its position.
