# Distillate: Toulmin's Warrants (Hitchcock 2003)

The carrier through which Toulmin's layout of arguments enters this vault, since no open copy of Toulmin 1958 was found. Hitchcock reconstructs the layout with page locators into the original and states what each element answers for, which is the vocabulary the site's claim-verification entry reaches for. Quotations come from the author's own hosted copy at McMaster University. Typographic quotation marks, apostrophes and dashes are normalised to their ASCII forms in the quotations.

## Core statements

- Toulmin replaced the premiss-and-conclusion vocabulary with six elements, claim, data or grounds, warrant, modal qualifier, rebuttal and backing. ^s1
  > "In The Uses of Argument (1958), Stephen Toulmin proposed a new, dialectically grounded structure for the layout of arguments, replacing the old terminology of \"premiss\" and \"conclusion\" with a new set of terms: claim, data (later \"grounds\"), warrant, modal qualifier, rebuttal, backing." (hitchcock-2003, opening paragraph)
- The layout is derived from the questions a challenger can put to an assertion, so each element answers a specific challenge. ^s2
  > "Toulmin articulated his proposal for the layout of arguments in the context of a single use of argument, that of justifying one's assertion in response to a challenge (Toulmin, 1958, 12). The proposed layout emerges from consideration of the questions that could arise in such a challenge." (hitchcock-2003, §1)
- The claim is the proposition asserted, and the first challenge asks what it rests on, which the data or grounds answer. ^s3
  > "Prior to the challenge, there must be an assertion, in which there is involved a claim, by which Toulmin appears to mean the proposition asserted. A challenger's first question in response to such an assertion is something like, \"What do you have to go on?\", to which the answer will be data (Toulmin, 1958, 97) or grounds" (hitchcock-2003, §1)
- The second challenge asks how the step from the grounds to the claim is licensed, which the warrant answers. ^s4
  > "But a challenger who accepts as correct the information given in answer to such a question can still ask a further question: \"How do you get there?\", to which the answer will be the warrant (Toulmin, 1958, 98; Toulmin, Rieke & Janik, 1984, 46). Whereas the data or grounds are the basis of the person's claim, the warrant is the person's justification for inferring the claim from those grounds." (hitchcock-2003, §1)
- A defeasible warrant carries a qualifier and conditions of rebuttal, and a challenge to the warrant itself is answered by its backing. ^s5
  > "Warrants may be qualified by such modal qualifiers as \"probably\" or \"generally\" or \"necessarily\" or \"presumably\", a fact generally reflected by qualifying the claim; if the warrant is defeasible, then conditions of exception or rebuttal may be mentioned. Finally, a challenger may ask for justification of the warrant, to which the answer will be a proposed backing for the warrant." (hitchcock-2003, §1)
- Grounds and warrant differ in function rather than in the sentence used, since the same sentence can supply information in one argument and license a step in another. ^s6
  > "Although the same universal sentence may be used in one context to state one's grounds for a claim and in another one's warrant for inferring a claim from grounds, the two statements will differ in their logical function." (hitchcock-2003, §1)

## Terms

- **Claim**: the proposition asserted, which the layout's other elements are called on to justify. [[20_distillates/publications/hitchcock-2003-toulmins-warrants#^s3]]
- **Grounds (data)**: what the claim rests on, given in answer to the challenge asking what one has to go on. [[20_distillates/publications/hitchcock-2003-toulmins-warrants#^s3]]
- **Warrant**: the general rule that licenses the step from grounds to claim. [[20_distillates/publications/hitchcock-2003-toulmins-warrants#^s4]]
- **Backing**: the justification offered for the warrant when the warrant itself is challenged. [[20_distillates/publications/hitchcock-2003-toulmins-warrants#^s5]]

## Open questions

- The layout is a scheme for the structure of a single argument. It fixes no record format, no verdict vocabulary and no procedure, so a document that records a claim, its evidence locator, its procedure and its verdict borrows the vocabulary and adds the bookkeeping; the addition is not carried by this source.
- Toulmin 1958 itself is quoted here at second hand. The page locators inside the quotations are Hitchcock's, and a claim resting on Toulmin's exact wording would need the book.
