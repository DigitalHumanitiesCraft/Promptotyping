# The nanopublication model makes a single statement citable by binding it to the annotations that carry its provenance and its assertion.

## Statement

Groth, Gibson and Velterop start from the observation that a statement can be validated only with its context, which a traditional publication supplies implicitly, and ask what must be added once the statement is separated from the document. Their model defines a statement as a uniquely identifiable triple, an annotation as a triple whose subject is a statement, and a nanopublication as the set of annotations referring to one statement together with an agreed minimum of them. The annotations carry provenance, where a statement came from, who imported it and who authored it, and an explicit assertion relation lets an entity claim the content. The requirements the model answers to are citability, attributability and reviewability, which is the closest prior art for a document that holds one claim with its evidence.
