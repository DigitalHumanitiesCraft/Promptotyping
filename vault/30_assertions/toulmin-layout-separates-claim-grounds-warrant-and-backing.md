# Toulmin's layout of arguments separates the claim, the grounds it rests on, the warrant licensing the step, and the backing of that warrant, each answering a specific challenge.

## Statement

Toulmin replaced premiss and conclusion with claim, data or grounds, warrant, modal qualifier, rebuttal and backing, and derived the layout from the questions a challenger can put to an assertion. The claim is the proposition asserted; the grounds answer what it rests on; the warrant answers how the step from grounds to claim is licensed; the backing answers a challenge to the warrant itself. Grounds and warrant differ in function rather than in wording, since the same sentence can supply information in one argument and license a step in another. The layout supplies the vocabulary for a document that records a claim with its evidence, and it fixes no record format, no verdict vocabulary and no procedure.
