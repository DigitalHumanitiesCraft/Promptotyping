# The literature describes a dialogue agent's behaviour as role play set by its prompt, and sharpens this into a simulator holding a distribution of characters rather than one fixed role.

## Statement

Shanahan, McDonell and Reynolds propose role play as the central concept for understanding dialogue-agent behaviour, with the role set by the prompt prepended to the exchange. The framing lets belief and goal vocabulary be used about the agent without asserting it in the human sense. They then correct the metaphor, since the agent commits to no well defined role in advance but generates a distribution of characters and refines it as the exchange proceeds, which they describe as a non-deterministic simulator producing simulacra. Role play is therefore the established name for what the site calls role simulation, and the correction bounds it, because a prompt-set role is a distribution the exchange keeps reshaping rather than an enforced boundary.
