# Interface typology

The typology sorts the browser-based interfaces of the documented projects by the epistemic function they serve, data format and visualisation technique having proved weaker as ordering principles. Section 4.2 carries the classification and its five categories. Verification Interfaces check pipeline outputs at defined milestones. Exploration Interfaces enable pattern discovery in structured research data through coordinated multiple views. Edition Interfaces render scholarly digital editions with facsimile synchronisation, TEI display, and editorial correction. Capture Interfaces enable structured input, annotation, or metadata creation, and in producing new data they shape the data model itself. Audit Interfaces make an entire research process inspectable alongside its results.

The ordering principle is taken from the epistemic forms of the learning-science literature, target knowledge structures paired with strategies for constructing them, and the typology operationalises that principle for research data interfaces. The five categories themselves are the paper's classification, and they are not mutually exclusive, since one pipeline project may combine several of them.

The classification addresses a gap the paper names across three traditions, the visual analytics process models, the research-activity taxonomies of the digital humanities, and the field's workflow registry, none of which classifies the artefacts that carry the work. Since the removal of a source on 2026-07-25 the Capture Interface has no external reference of its own.
