# Document typology

The typology sorts Promptotyping Documents by the kind of knowledge they hold. Declarative Documents hold knowledge about the subject matter, Process Documents hold knowledge about the course of the work, and Action Documents hold knowledge about how to act. Section 3.3 carries the definition, and all three are knowledge documents specialised by that criterion.

The typology is analytical and without rigid boundaries, and it earns its place through a diagnostic function. Factually incorrect output points to the Declarative Documents, formally wrong output to the Action Documents, and unclear decision logic to the Process Documents.

The cut was set on 2026-07-25, and the declarative subtype was renamed from Knowledge Documents in the same pass. Two ordering principles were examined and rejected, the tense, because a plan lies in the future and is no process document, and the addressing, because it turns every binding statement into an action document. The derivation from knowledge engineering that Section 3.3 invokes still lacks its citation.
