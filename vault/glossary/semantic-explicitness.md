# Semantic explicitness

Semantic explicitness is the first of the two dimensions in which data quality bears on generation, the degree to which a source carries its meaning in a form a machine can work from. Simply structured formats such as CSV and JSON are the minimum condition, standard-conformant formats carry more meaning, and in ontology-based data the meaning of the data is itself machine-readable. The more semantics the data carries, the less the method's context layer must supply. Section 2.3 carries the definition.

The counter-dimension is [[glossary/token-economy]], which orders the same formats in reverse. The term is the paper's own; its supporting parts, among them the FAIR principles and the documented reconstruction of structure from human-readable layout, carry their own sources.
